## Matera in December: the Sassi in Christmas mode

It takes about an hour from Bari (1h-1h15 by motorhome). Not much, for a place that feels like another world. In December Matera changes light and pace. It's the right time for a **long weekend of 3-4 days** by motorhome. For example Thursday 17 to Sunday 20 December, or the weekend of the 12th-13th.

The Christmas Village opened on 20 November with the big switch-on of the "La luce del Mediterraneo" lights. The official programme places it in the Parco del Boschetto: check the location before you go. From 4 December until the Epiphany the Christmas markets return to piazza Vittorio Veneto, via La Vista and via Don Minzoni. Final dates and the end-of-year break should be checked on the Comune di Matera website.

## Living nativity and music in the neighbourhoods

The highlight is the Living Nativity in the Sasso Caveoso. This is the 16th edition, with a route of about 1.5 km and over 200 performers. It opens on Friday 4 December. Performances take place on December weekends (5-6, 12-13, 19-20, 26-27) and on 2-3 January: dates to be checked on the official website. Tickets are sold online through the official channels of the Living Nativity. It's a paid event, so check price and times before you leave.

There are also the "Suoni nei vicinati" concerts. Students of the Duni Conservatory play on Wednesdays and Sundays, from 5:30 to 8:30 pm, in the small squares of Santa Maria de Armenis and San Pietro Barisano. The "RacCunto" festival runs from 20 to 23 December.

## Practical tips for motorhome travellers

- **Where to park**: choose an equipped motorhome area near the centre and get around on foot or by public transport. The lanes of the Sassi aren't made for motorhomes. Check availability and rules before you go.
- **What to see**: Sasso Caveoso and Sasso Barisano, the rock churches and the viewpoint over the gorge, on the Murgia Materana side. At dawn, with fewer people, it's a different story.
- **A stop in Gravina**: Gravina in Puglia, on the road between Bari and Matera, is worth a stop. The old town sits on the edge of its canyon, and there's the aqueduct bridge. Another stop in stone.
- **Cold**: December evenings are chilly. The Kentucky has diesel heating, and the inverter helps if you park without hook-up.
