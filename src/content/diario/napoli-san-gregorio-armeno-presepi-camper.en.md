In Naples there is a street that smells of cork, glue and terracotta at Christmas: **via San Gregorio Armeno**. Master nativity artisans display hand-painted shepherds, scenery and figurines here. Every year they also sculpt the faces of the moment, in miniature. A bit of Neapolitan self-mockery, in the place where the nativity scene is a very serious matter.

The workshops are open all year. The nativity fair itself runs until Sunday 10 January 2027. From Bari it is about 3 hours of driving, depending on traffic. That makes the ideal trip **4-5 days**, between a long weekend and a short week: enough time to drive calmly, see Naples without rushing and come home with a bag full of shepherds.

## Planning the trip
Naples' old town is a ZTL (restricted traffic zone), so no camper among the alleys. Before you leave, check the current rules on the City of Naples website and plan to visit on foot or by public transport. If you can, pick a weekday: the crowds are much smaller and you can stop at every window without being pushed along.

## What to see and do
Step inside the workshops and watch the masters at work: that is the real show. Then hunt for terracotta shepherds, cork grottoes and miniatures: tiny baskets of fruit, fish, cured meats. Every workshop has its own style and story.

From there you are a short walk from via dei Tribunali and Spaccanapoli, perfect for a pizza break. Spend the other days in Pozzuoli and the Campi Flegrei, free to move whenever you like.
